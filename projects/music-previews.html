Opening up music exploration

Does a richer preview help people choose better, or encourage them to explore more?

Dajin Lee, Seungmoon Choi, and Oliver Schneider

ACM CHI 2027 · Under review

Manuscript under review at CHI 2027.

Watch demo

Methods and focus

  • Multimodal interaction
  • Experience and decisions
  • Field evaluation

The question

A short excerpt can determine whether an unfamiliar track receives further attention. This project asks how additional sensory and semantic cues change that early encounter, and how its value should be evaluated.

The system and study

Audio, text, vibration, and their combinations are evaluated in a controlled laboratory study with 48 participants and a six-day field deployment with 60 participants. PRISM organizes measures of experience, interaction effort, discrimination, and willingness to explore.

What we learned

In the submitted studies, richer previews broadened exploration more consistently than they improved discrimination between later-valued and later-rejected tracks. Vibration supported absorption and exploratory listening; text increased perceived sufficiency without a stable gain in discrimination.
